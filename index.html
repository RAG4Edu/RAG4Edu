Erasmus+ Cooperation Partnership · 2026–2029

Helping educators build trustworthy AI for their own courses

RAG4EDU equips higher-education teachers to design Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) applications grounded in their own course materials, with an open curriculum, hands-on training, accessible tools and a European community of practice.

Why RAG4EDU

Generic chatbots are not enough for teaching

Generative AI is entering lecture halls quickly, but it is often used ad hoc and without a link to what is actually taught. That raises fair questions about reliability, bias, transparency and data protection.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation connects AI to sources the educator chooses, such as lecture notes, readings, slides or lab guides, so answers stay grounded in the course and can be traced back to them. Most educators we asked are interested, but few feel ready to build such tools. RAG4EDU is here to change that.

What we do

We combine research on teaching and learning with practical tools, so that any educator can design RAG support that fits their course.

About the project

About RAG4EDU

Building educators’ AI competence in designing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for teaching and learning in higher education.

The challenge

Large language models are quickly finding their way into higher education, yet they are often used ad hoc, with generic prompts and little connection to the knowledge a course is built on. Educators rightly worry about reliability, accuracy, bias, transparency and data protection.

What RAG changes

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) combines generative AI with sources the educator curates: lecture notes, slides, readings, exams or lab materials. The AI looks up relevant passages before it answers, so its support stays grounded in the course, is easier to check, and remains under the educator's control.

Our approach

RAG4EDU starts from pedagogy. Together, the partners are developing:

  • a competence-based RAG curriculum with clear learning outcomes and flexible pathways,
  • an open toolbox with templates and worked examples for building course-specific assistants,
  • a modular training programme, piloted with educators in several countries, and
  • a European Community of Practice that keeps the work going beyond the project.

Responsible, European AI

The project favours European language models and infrastructure, privacy-by-design and open licences, and it follows the EU AI Act. All main results will be published openly so that institutions across Europe can reuse and adapt them.

Grounded in educators' needs

Before the project began, the partners surveyed educators in seven language contexts. Interest in RAG was high, but confidence was low: people asked for practical examples, templates, time-efficient training and support with data protection. RAG4EDU is designed around those needs.
